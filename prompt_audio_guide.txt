----------------------------------------------------------------------
I'm preparing for [the Ausbildung zur Medizinischen Technologin für
Laboratoriumsanalytik]. I haven't studied chemistry or biology in years.
Write a complete study guide on the topics below, so I can listen to it
as an audio book while walking. A local app reads the text aloud.

LANGUAGE: [English / German]

- English: plain, clear English only. No German words, no German terms,
  no translations.
- German: German at B1 level only. No English words in the text. Short,
  clear sentences, mostly 8 to 18 words. Use the present tense wherever
  possible.

TOPICS (in this order):
[Paste the topic list here. Example:
CHEMISTRY (general): atomic structure, the periodic table, electron
shells, isotopes; chemical bonding (ionic, covalent, polar, hydrogen
bonds); the mole, molar mass, Avogadro's number; chemical equations and
stoichiometry (balancing, limiting reagents); states of matter,
solutions, solubility; concentration units (mol/L, g/L, %, ppm);
reaction types (precipitation, redox with oxidation numbers, acid-base).
BIOLOGY (cell biology): prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells; organelles and
their functions; the cell membrane and transport (diffusion, osmosis,
active transport); the cell cycle; mitosis vs meiosis; DNA, RNA and
protein synthesis (transcription and translation) at a working level.
LAB MATHS: the SI system and unit conversion; ratios, proportions,
percentages; and an introduction to dilution problems (C1V1 = C2V2).]

WHAT THE GUIDE SHOULD BE

- A teaching text, not a story. No characters, no dialogue, no quiz
  questions. Speak directly to the listener.
- Length: about [150] minutes of audio. Allow about 110 words per
  minute: 60 minutes is about 6,500 words, 150 minutes about 16,500.
  Stay within 10% of the target.
- One chapter per concept, in the order of the topic list, so that each
  idea builds on the ones before it. Split a big topic into two chapters
  rather than rushing it.
- Start with a short chapter on how to use the guide. Begin each part
  (for example Chemistry, Cell biology, Lab maths) with a short intro
  chapter, and end it with a summary chapter. Finish with a final
  summary of the whole guide.
- Every chapter has the same structure:
  1. Explain the concept from zero, in plain language, assuming no prior
     knowledge. Define every technical term the first time it appears.
  2. A paragraph that starts with "Why does this matter in the lab?"
     (German: "Warum ist das im Labor wichtig?") with a concrete,
     realistic example from the medical laboratory.
  3. A last paragraph that starts with "In short:" (German: "Kurz
     gesagt:") and sums up the chapter in two to four sentences.
- Use simple everyday comparisons (kitchen, house, post office,
  cookbook, bus) when they help. Do not use comparisons from computers,
  programming or IT.
- Worked examples with numbers are welcome. Say every step of the
  calculation in words.
- Facts must be correct. Use typical reference values and say "about"
  or "normally" when values differ between labs.

HOW THE APP READS THE TEXT (this decides the format)

- Plain UTF-8 text, no markdown: no *, bullet points or numbered lists.
  The only special character is # at the start of a chapter heading line
  (see below).
- An empty line starts a new paragraph, which gets a 1.5 s pause. Every
  sentence gets a 0.6 s pause after it. A line break without an empty
  line is ignored.
- Sentences are split at . ! ? or … followed by a space. So every
  sentence must end with one of those.
- Every chapter starts with a heading line: # , then a short title of
  one to four words that says what the chapter is about. No chapter
  number, no period at the end. Put an empty line before it.
  English guide, title only:

  # Atomic structure

  German guide, German title, space, em dash (—), space, English
  translation (only the German part is read aloud; the player shows the
  English under it):

  # Der Atombau — Atomic structure

- Make a new chapter every 3 to 6 minutes of audio (about 350 to 650
  words).

LIMITS OF THE VOICE GENERATOR (Supertonic text-to-speech)

The text is read aloud exactly as written. An unknown character makes that
sentence fail. An abbreviation also puts a false pause in the middle of a
sentence. So:

- No digits at all. Write numbers as words: "seven point four" or
  "sieben komma vier", "one hundred forty millimoles per liter" or
  "hundertvierzig Millimol pro Liter".
- Large and small numbers in words too: "six point zero two times ten to
  the power of twenty-three" or "sechs komma null zwei mal zehn hoch
  dreiundzwanzig".
- No abbreviations with a period: not "e.g.", "i.e.", "etc.", "approx.",
  "Dr.", "z. B.", "usw.", "bzw.", "ca.", "Nr." or "d. h.". Write them
  out. A period followed by a capital letter always ends a sentence.
- Letter abbreviations that should be spelled out get spaces between the
  letters: "A T P", "S I", "p p m". Common ones like DNA, RNA, PCR and
  pH can stay as they are.
- No chemical formulas, subscripts or superscripts (not H₂O, not CO₂).
  Write names: "water", "carbon dioxide" or "Wasser", "Kohlendioxid".
  Describe equations in words: "two molecules of hydrogen and one
  molecule of oxygen give two molecules of water".
- No unit symbols (not mg/L, °C, µl or %). Write them out: "milligrams
  per liter", "degrees Celsius", "microliters", "percent".
- Formulas in words: "c one times V one equals c two times V two".
- No arrows, Greek letters, ±, ×, ≈, =, +, brackets, slashes or emoji.
- Don't write "..." in the middle of a sentence; it ends the sentence
  there.
- Characters tested and accepted: letters, ä ö ü Ä Ö Ü ß, and
  , . : ; - – — … ! ? ' " “ ” ‘ ’ » « € % &

OUTPUT

Plain text only, no code block and no commentary. The first line is the
title, ending with a period. Because the text is long, write it in parts
of about [6] chapters each. After each part I'll say "continue".

AFTER THE CHAT (for you, not for Claude)

Run these in the StudyAudio folder (C:\LOCAL FILES\Claude Code\Python\StudyAudio) (see README.md).

1. Paste all parts into one file and save it as UTF-8 (Notepad does this by
   default). End the name with the language: e.g. phase2_guide_en.txt or
   phase2_guide_de.txt. Keep the empty lines between paragraphs.
2. Check it:     python -m guideaudio phase2_guide_en.txt --check
   (It lists every error with its line number, and the length of each
   chapter. A build won't start while there are errors.)
3. Preview:      python -m guideaudio phase2_guide_en.txt --limit 20
   (Writes out/phase2_guide_en_preview.mp3.)
4. Build it:     python -m guideaudio phase2_guide_en.txt
   (Writes out/phase2_guide_en.mp3, read with the English or German voice
   to match the file name.)
